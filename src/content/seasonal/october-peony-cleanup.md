# October: break the peony disease cycle

Late-season peony spots may look cosmetic, especially on an established plant that flowered well. The damaged leaf will not become healthy again. The October opportunity is to reduce the infected material carried into next spring.

## Wait for the season to finish

After frost or natural dieback, cut herbaceous peony stems near the ground without damaging the crown. Collect spotted leaves, fallen fragments, and old stems rather than leaving them as mulch around the plant.

Do not put badly infected material into a cool home compost pile. Use the disposal method available and permitted locally. Clean tools before moving to healthy plants.

## Reset the site

- remove weeds and volunteer growth that crowd the crown;
- keep mulch from piling directly over the crown;
- note whether irrigation wets the foliage;
- mark plants that need more space or better airflow next year;
- avoid a heavy late nitrogen feeding;
- leave a durable label so spring observations refer to the same clump.

Sanitation is not sterilization. Spores can arrive from beyond the bed, and wet weather still matters. The goal is lower pressure and healthy foliage for longer, not a promise of zero spots.
