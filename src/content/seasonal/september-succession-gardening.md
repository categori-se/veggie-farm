# September: make one more small sowing

September is not August with cooler air. Days shorten quickly, the sun sits lower, and a crop with “30 days to maturity” may take longer than it did in spring.

The useful response is not to stop planting altogether. It is to make smaller, quicker, more deliberate bets.

## Favor flexible harvests

Prioritize crops that can be useful before full maturity:

- arugula, mizuna, spinach, and lettuce as baby leaf;
- radishes and small turnips;
- beet and turnip greens;
- young kale, chard, bok choy, and collard leaves.

Garlic belongs to a later fall establishment cycle, not this quick-harvest group. September is a good time to choose and prepare its bed.

## Succession as risk management

Sow a short row, label it, and repeat only if conditions remain useful. Two small sowings separated by 10–14 days can reveal whether heat, rain, or cooling soil changes emergence.

| Condition | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Soil remains hot | Shade lightly and favor heat-tolerant greens |
| Surface dries quickly | Water gently and check daily before emergence |
| Nights cool sharply | Use row cover over established seedlings |
| Frost runway is short | Harvest younger; avoid slow full-size goals |

## Protection has limits

Row cover can moderate cold and wind. It cannot restore daylight or make a late seedling grow at summer speed. Put protection over healthy, established plants before damaging weather rather than using it as a rescue plan.

<div class="field-notes">
  <p>Compare two sowings: date, soil temperature, emergence time, thinning, cover use, and harvest stage. “The second spinach sowing emerged faster after the soil cooled” is the kind of note that improves next September.</p>
</div>
