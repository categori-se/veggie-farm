# July: harvest cucumbers for quality, not size

A cucumber is eaten while botanically immature. That is why a fruit can remain on the vine, become much larger, turn yellow, and still not be “rotten.” It has simply moved toward mature seed—and away from the texture most people want at the table.

## Read the fruit

Pick before the variety's normal eating size is greatly exceeded.

| Better eating stage | Moving toward seed maturity |
|---|---|
| Even green color | Yellow or yellow-orange skin |
| Firm flesh | Soft or coarse flesh |
| Small, tender seeds | Large, hard seeds |
| Compact seed cavity | Wide, watery seed cavity |
| Fresh flavor | Flat or bitter flavor |

Exact size varies. A pickling cucumber, slicer, and long Asian cucumber do not share one harvest length. The seed packet and a few taste tests are more useful than a universal number.

## Make harvest easy

- check vines every day or two during peak production;
- look under leaves and behind the trellis;
- cut or twist fruit without tearing the vine;
- harvest misshapen fruit while it is still useful;
- remove fruit that has gone fully yellow.

Allowing one cucumber to mature seed can redirect the plant's energy and reduce continued production. Regular picking is part of crop care, not merely collection.

  <p>Record the cultivar, size at best flavor, harvest interval, and how quickly fruit becomes seedy. Next year's harvest rule should come from the variety in your garden.</p>
