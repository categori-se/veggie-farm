# Garden Glossary

## Annual

A plant that completes its life cycle in one growing season.

## Perennial

A plant that returns for multiple years.

## Mulch

Material placed on the soil surface to protect soil, reduce evaporation, suppress weeds, and moderate temperature.

## Compost

Decomposed organic matter used to improve soil.

## Hardening off

The process of gradually acclimating indoor-grown plants to outdoor conditions.

## Succession planting

Planting small amounts repeatedly to extend harvests.

## Biennial

A plant whose life cycle normally spans two growing seasons, often growing leaves first and flowering later. Parsley is commonly grown for its first-season leaves.

## Bolting

The move from leafy growth toward a flowering stem. For a leaf crop, record when it begins and the conditions around it rather than assuming fertilizer will reverse it.

## Direct sowing and transplanting

Direct sowing places seed where the crop will grow. Transplanting moves an established seedling there. Check which starting point a variety uses for its days-to-maturity estimate.

## Frost date and hardiness zone

A frost date is a seasonal planning reference, not a weather guarantee. A hardiness zone describes winter cold and helps with perennial selection; it is not a vegetable planting calendar. [Understand the distinction](/content/garden/understanding-your-climate).

## Plant spacing and row spacing

Plant spacing describes distance between plants; row spacing describes distance between rows. Both affect access and the layout. The [spacing guide](/content/reference/plant-spacing) explains how mature habit changes the decision.

## Observation and inference

An observation is what you saw or measured. An inference is a possible explanation. “Leaves wilted before noon” is an observation; “the roots need more water” requires checking.

## Unknown

Information you do not have. In a planting record, unknown crop history is different from a known empty bed. Preserve that difference when comparing rotation or timing advice.

## Use the terms

[Read a crop guide](/content/vegetables/), [compare planting decisions](/tools/today), or [write a field note](/tools/my-garden). Use ordinary language in your notes; precision matters more than jargon.
