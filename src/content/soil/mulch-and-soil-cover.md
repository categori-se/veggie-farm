# Mulch and Soil Cover

A bed between crops still needs a plan. Soil cover helps protect the surface while you decide what will grow next; it should also leave you able to sow, water and inspect the bed.

## Match the cover to the next task

| Your next task | Decision to make | What to check afterward |
|---|---|---|
| Sow a small-seeded crop | Keep the sowing strip accessible | Can you see emergence and reach weeds? |
| Maintain established plants | Cover exposed soil while retaining access | Does water reach the soil below? |
| Leave a bed between seasons | Choose mulch or a suitable cover crop | Who will remove or manage it before planting? |
| Walk between beds | Keep the route distinct from growing space | Is footing usable after rain? |

Cover reduces erosion and evaporation and moderates soil temperature. Organic materials also change as they decompose, so inspect what is happening beneath them. [Maryland Extension's soil-health guidance](https://www.extension.umd.edu/resource/improve-soil-health-climate-resilient-garden) describes mulch, living roots and reduced disturbance as complementary practices.

## Inspect before adding another layer

Lift a small section after watering or rain. Record whether the surface below is dry, moist or persistently wet and whether weeds are growing through it. Compare that observation with an uncovered spot, noting differences in shade and soil. This is a garden observation, not a controlled experiment.

## Close the seasonal loop

Record material, approximate depth, date and the next intended crop. At planting time, note the effort needed to clear a seed row. A cover that saves summer weeding but makes spring sowing difficult may need a different place in your system.
